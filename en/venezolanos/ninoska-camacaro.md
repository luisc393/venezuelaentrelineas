Some voices seem to have no ceiling. Ninoska Camacaro's is one of them: a coloratura soprano, the voice type that lives on opera's highest notes and fastest passages, the one that has to sing Mozart's Queen of the Night without letting the vertigo show.

She began singing as a little girl, and at four or five she was already giving her first concert. For five years she studied Business Administration and music at the same time, in a musical education that took nine years in all. She started at the Juan José Landaeta Conservatory with Gisella Höllander, then moved to the Simón Bolívar Conservatory, where she graduated from the class of Sara Catarine. She says it took her years to rebuild her vocal technique, a patience that says a great deal about her discipline.

Her orchestral debut came in 2008: a Mozart gala at the Aula Magna, with the Caracas Municipal Symphony and conductor Rodolfo Saglimbeni. She sang an aria of the Queen of the Night and the duet of Papageno and Papagena. At the Aula Magna she has also sung the soprano solos of *Carmina Burana*, a work she has returned to several times.

That perseverance paid off on the great stages of Caracas. In 2018 she sang the title role in *Lucia di Lammermoor* at the Teatro Municipal, a part she had dreamed of since 2011, and the following year she returned to that theater as Gilda in *Rigoletto*. With the Teresa Carreño Theater Opera Company she debuted as Adina in *L'elisir d'amore*, her first opera in the Ríos Reyna Hall. In 2024 she sang Violetta in *La Traviata* in the Ríos Reyna Hall of the Teresa Carreño Theater, and the critic of *Últimas Noticias* wrote that she “stole the show”. Today she is an active member of the Teresa Carreño Theater Opera Company and has sung with the country's leading orchestras, under conductors such as Alfredo Rugeles, Rodolfo Saglimbeni and Elisa Vegas.

Between productions, Ninoska keeps betting on chamber recitals, often free of charge, in venues such as CVA Las Mercedes and the Salón Siglo XXI. It is her way of bringing lyric music closer to people, and of showing that opera is still sung in Venezuela, and sung very well.
