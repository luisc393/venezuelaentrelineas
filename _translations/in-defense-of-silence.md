> "When people have nothing clear to say about something, instead of keeping quiet they usually do the opposite: they SPEAK in the superlative, that is, they shout."
>
> (Ortega y Gasset)

> "When the heart is full, words do not flow."
>
> (Tagore)

The horses of the rain have been rehearsing a gallop on the roof for some time now. Outside, in the street around us, the honking goes on endlessly. Cars fight over a scrap of concrete, arguing at the top of their voices, roaring like gorillas in the remotest jungle. Every so often a clap of thunder silences the audience. It seems that the fruitless howling gets on the nerves even of the rain clouds. Little by little, to the rhythm of the hours of waiting, the clatter of hooves grows more distant, and the gallop of the rain fades into a faint mist over the endless street. My room, where I am writing now, has gone quiet. Has gone quiet...

All this has brought back the weary school days of the rainy season. A window open to a gray sky, the room full of sickly light, and all of us drowsy at our silent wooden desks. Silence fell. I was left with the word on my lips. Silence fell. Why silence? What silence? What do we know of it, dark companion of the night?

Silence has disappeared from our world. Who hid it? Who is the murderer? The truth is that the twentieth century is a century of much talking, much publishing, an atmosphere full of commercials, and so on and so forth. The value of opinion, of what people say, today ranks among the highest of human values. Silence, on the other hand, is a devalued currency. In that immense stock exchange that is public opinion, shares in speech have risen to fabulous prices, while shares in silence have hit rock bottom. There is deflation of silence and a thunderous inflation of talk. And since (with a great business mind) no one risks bankruptcy by buying phantom shares, silence is on the dung heap; but is it even there...?

Until recently, night was the refuge of this persecuted character, now almost extinct. Night was its hideout, its quiet backwater. Thinkers and poets hid there. Lovers took refuge there, wanting to open their hearts and let love distill little by little. There the mother sang to her child with a silent throat and a full heart. Today we have sold the darkness, the silent night, at the slave market, for the price of cheap music.

*[Illustration: silhouettes of human figures beside a building with a tower]*

Man (and silence suggests these reflections to us) is the most curious of animals. For a very simple reason: because he thinks. This also means that if man does not think, he turns into an animal of the worst kind, inferior by his very makeup to almost all the others. If any of the geniuses, those men who have sharpened the blade of their minds to the utmost, had been unable to think at a given moment of their lives, what would have become of them? I can already hear the barkers: "Step right up, ladies and gentlemen; step right up and see the strangest animal Mother Earth has ever borne. Come in; it doesn't climb, it doesn't charge, it's almost hairless. Come in, come in: we have found the missing link, and you can see it for only so much." And this, which we can imagine and which makes us laugh just to picture it, is how many people live today. Thought is becoming too expensive an item in terms of effort. And we must be clear that human thought is not truly thought unless it is directed toward Truth, unless it dwells in it. Otherwise it turns into a collection of trivial notions, more fitting for any other creature than for a man. Or do we not realize that man is in danger of ceasing to be man? Do we not see that the one who repeats slogans on every subject is nothing but a featherless parrot? And a parrot, moreover, that sometimes is not only unpleasant but shouts threats and insults. This is our risk, and we must be aware of it. Only then will we restore the values that have been devalued by the shallowness of those who play the stock market of public opinion.

We must reclaim silence. Reclaim it by making it our own lived experience, each one of us in particular. In this way we will keep our words from being mere straws thrown to the wind. If, as Ortega says, "words are words when they are said by someone to someone", we must take care not only to address some person when we speak, but also to be persons ourselves, truly, with all our human character. If periods of silence alternate with periods of speech in our lives, our lives will have a rhythm and can become ever more human. From the "speaking" periods we will draw material for the "silent" ones, and from the latter, weightier and more balanced ideas and judgments, able to raise the quality of all our chatter.

Silence, however, is something more than not being surrounded by noise. It is more than a pause between two noisy moments, a kind of intermission between two tiring days of work. We must understand it as an activity, or a moment of activity, on a par with the noisy one. In this way we give it a certain substance, because with it we mark a stage of that very tangible reality which is human life, and above all intellectual life; and in this way, too, we raise its worth to a higher level. This also requires us to review whatever idea of silence we may have. Silence demands that we ourselves not be full of noise; it demands that we not talk so much with our own self. Only by cutting off the constant waterfall of conversation, with others or with ourselves, can we hear the muffled murmur of the deep life that stirs within us.

Tagore said: "When the heart is full, words do not flow", and with this phrase he shows us a new side of the subject we wish to look at, however lightly. Silence is the language of love. One who loves does not speak in words; he speaks with his presence, with his silence. The dialogue of love is therefore the opposite of any other: here sentences serve as an interlude, a release, between two moments of deep and intimate communion. At most they are a small bridge that can carry, in a few strokes, the whole intense charge of life that builds up behind them. Love speaks in "silent conversation", and each word, of the few that spring up in the course of the dialogue, simple though it may be, and shallow to anyone who heard it in other circumstances, is charged with new emotional content, with new vital and authentically human content; they are floodgates opened to let out the whole torrent of love held behind the dam of our inner life.

With all of this, we have tried to establish the idea that the word truly comes into its own when it is backed by the content of thought. And that silence is the means, the vehicle, for reaching this goal of making man more human, and then for raising him beyond the merely human plane.

*rafael t. caldera pietri*
