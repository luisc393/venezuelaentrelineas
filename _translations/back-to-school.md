*We are back in touch with our readers in this fourth year of Leer Entre Líneas. In its effort to help form the families who read it, the paper tries to address the ordinary things that happen in every home, so that we can reflect on them a little and put more educational purpose into what we do as parents.*

For *Leer Entre Líneas*, the family's work as educator is the most important thing of all. Today parents need to study, prepare and plan their children's education, because the world around us is full of outside influences that break into the privacy of the family and can undo our best efforts at education.

That is why it matters so much that parents take great care in forming their children. Specifically, in building good working habits, which in the end means developing human virtues. And that is achieved through concrete actions that parents must carry out, paying attention to what each child in particular needs.

In short, it is about exercising our role as parents a little more, knowing that we will succeed to the extent that we prepare ourselves better.

Within this duty to educate, it is worth remembering that the start of the school year is a start for the whole family, not just for the children. Although we will not be going to class, we know that how well our children do at school depends partly on us.

If we have not yet dropped the mindset of being the school's "customers", now is the time to do it. According to that way of thinking, the school bears all the responsibility for the child's studies. It is when parents demand good results without having worked beforehand with the teachers and with their children to achieve them. It is the attitude of fathers and mothers who think "I have enough on my plate" and "that's why I pay the school so much". They forget that part of what is on their plate, and a very important part, is precisely their children's education, for which we are the ones primarily responsible.

## How to go about it

Many things contribute to children doing well at school. But one comes first. If we parents do not take an interest in our children's studies, they may not take much interest either. If we never look at a notebook or a folder, never answer a question, never ask how a special lesson or an exam went, never suggest a book to help prepare a topic, it is only logical that our child will think we don't care. And if we do these things in a bad mood, grudgingly or without interest, it is even worse. Besides missing a chance to share, we will be dramatically discouraging that child.

Then we think we have the right to demand good grades, and we get furious at a failing mark. And then come the punishments and threats, without stopping to consider the reasons behind those small failures.

## The atmosphere is essential

We parents are responsible for the atmosphere at home when our children come back from school. After a short rest, they should find a setting that helps them start their homework.

If there is no control at all over what they do, if the television can be on at any hour, if life together is not pleasant, it is hard for them to settle down to study.

## Don't do their homework

"Going back to class ourselves" does not mean doing our children's homework. They must face their own difficulties, and we must give them the tools that help them. For that we need to keep our own general knowledge up to date, since it may be put to the test more than once.

We should also keep an eye out for the first signs of difficulty with a topic or a subject and go to the school to talk with the teachers or tutors, who will surely point out things we may not have considered. That way we can keep an early difficulty from turning into a failed course.

## The heart of education

Parents should give priority to the effort and dedication their children put into their studies. We must not be soft or give in. Because it is precisely in their studies that they will have the chance to develop many human virtues. It is in their "profession" for this stage of life that they will practice hard work, fortitude, patience, responsibility, order, cheerfulness and so on.

That is why studying, and teaching them through their homework to do things well, is the very heart of education.

If our children do not make an effort in what is their ordinary occupation, they lose their best chance to grow as people.

This is a key moment, with the school year just beginning, to tell them that we expect great things of them. To fire their enthusiasm and keep it alive. It does not matter how well or badly they did before. Now, starting over, they can do better.

Keep talking with them and encouraging them. You will be surprised at the results.
