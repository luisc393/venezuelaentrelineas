*Reflections before a door*

*[Illustration: an open door with a flowerpot on the threshold.]*

"Go to Seville and lose your chair," as the Spanish saying goes, and that is exactly what happened to me with this door. I went to Europe and, quick as you like, other names began to peek through it. It means that the door of VÉRTICE is wide. Many of us fit on its threshold. But I came back, and here I am at my post again; what's more, determined not to be pushed aside a second time. Between you and me, there was a little taking of liberties, but happily, trust reigns in the team that makes this magazine. So I didn't get angry, and without protest I let others take hold of the doorknob. But that's it, it's over. I promise you that from now on, if some cheeky fellow wants to look in, I might, at most, let him open a shutter, because I am not handing my whole door over to anyone else ("Famous last words").

So then, before the door of VÉRTICE we are going to reflect on, or perhaps comment on, the year that begins in its pages. If the reader has already leafed through this issue and the previous one, you will have noticed women's names appearing here and there at the foot of the articles. It is a New Year's resolution: from now on, VÉRTICE is to have a women's team at work. This was needed. Today's University is full of women who are after something more than knowing how to sew and sing. A university magazine without the collaboration of our sex limps along; it does not fully express itself.

There is a lot of talk these days about "women's journalism", and the other day, chatting with my teammates, we concluded that we didn't know exactly what it meant. I think people associate it somewhat with recipes, fashion and advice columns. Ah, but no! We are not going to put any of that in VÉRTICE, unless Venezuela's university women unanimously request it. And I don't think they will. In VÉRTICE we are going to speak, in women's voices, about whatever comes up and is of general interest. That will be our "women's journalism". There is no doubt that our pieces will have their own special touch, which is just what we want. We women see things differently in many cases, and that difference is precisely "the salt of the earth". In fact we are interested in everything, but sometimes we see that everything from angles different from the viewpoint of the "uglier sex". So then, through the door of the magazine a different breath has just come in, but one aimed at the same goal: to do good journalism.

I ought to end these reflections without adding anything else, but since I have been silent for so long, I give in to the temptation to put in one more little word. You have to seize the moment by the forelock; in this case I am dragging it in a little by the hair, since we are talking about feminine differences. Very soon after getting back, I landed, out of the blue, in a course in a certain Faculty that I had better not name. I noticed two things. First, that students have picked up the dreadful habit of murmuring, about who knows what, while the professor is giving his lecture, and that the voices of the murmuring are often the deep voices of the corresponding sex. It used to be said that we women were the talkers; we still are, but now the men imitate us. Only vices are catching. The other thing is that spelling (I have proof in a good number of exams I gave) is becoming more and more a feminine heritage. The things the boys wrote for me! And what does this mean? That absent-mindedness is now masculine? So I want to turn my final reflection into a concrete resolution to propose to students for the year now beginning: talk less and write better. In other words, mouth shut and eyes open. I know many professors will thank me for it. Don't mention it.

*Alicia Álamo Bartolomé*
