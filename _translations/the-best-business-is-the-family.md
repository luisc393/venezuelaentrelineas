A few days ago a saleswoman came to see me to offer me "the best business of my life". Its benefits were so spectacular that she gave me the example of a Japanese man who had earned 120 million bolívares in a single week. "It will sort out your life forever," the saleswoman told me, though deep down she knew that her pitch sounded dazzling but a little out of touch with reality.

She sold me the idea so well that, without thinking much, I decided to join. Thank goodness the clever saleswoman did not make me take out my money right then! When my euphoria began to fade, I stopped for a second to think. "Well, but when could I ever work on this business, if absolutely all my time goes to my family and to projects that, one way or another, are tied to it?"

And that was when I realized that the best business I had was my family.

And truly it is the biggest and most wonderful business, one that never crossed my mind in my university days.

It is now, as the mother of four children, that I realize my life is a constant effort to build the best business in the world: forming intelligent and capable people; with very high human values; eager to work and to study; with great faith in God and therefore great hope in life; always willing to help and to serve others.

In a few words: forming people of integrity, all of one piece, with the strength to live in the world they were given and which they must help to improve.

And it is at times like Christmas that you see your business is running excellently. It is at Christmas that you notice how, unlike many children who ask the Child Jesus for a television for their room, a Nintendo, a machine gun or a SuperTrack car, your five-year-old asks for a "real" carpentry set (not a plastic one) because he wants to be a carpenter when he grows up, or your four-year-old asks for a bookshop because he dreams of making and selling books when he grows up.

That is when you understand that your children cannot be left in the hands of strangers (read: hired help), who, however good they may be, will find it very hard to prepare themselves and to give knowledge and love the way a mother can.

In the end the saleswoman told me: "Well, if you want to, you can make some time for this business. I do it, and I have children."

And I answered: "If I ever had any time to spare, it would always go, first, to spoiling my husband, who is the only 'spoiled one' among 'my children'; second, to being with my children and giving them the very best of my love and preparation; third, to preparing myself with reading, talks, conferences and so on that help me build my family better; and fourth, to trying some business that necessarily has to do with its education."

I am convinced that the great business, worldwide, is the family. But most people have not realized it.

Many people, in pursuit of great financial deals, have put their family in second place, not knowing that in a future not too far away they will begin to wonder whether it was worth no longer giving time to their home and their children because, in money terms, they were not profitable.

If only no one ever had to ask that question; in the end we might find that wonderful world that parents always dream of for their children.
