A culture of service is a human disposition to truly serve. To carry that disposition in one's mind and heart is to get past the barrier of favoritism; it is to treat people for who they are and not for what they "have": a cell phone, a car, a tie, an image, a credit card, money in the bank, and so on. It means making no distinctions by sex, race, dress or social and economic standing.

The culture of service is a more human way of doing and acting. It is acting on a conviction: when I deal with another human being, I can make that person a little happier through my readiness to attend to them, to help solve "their" problem, which deep down is "my" problem. To serve is to do something good, a good deed, for another. It is to contribute something of value. And doing something good for someone else fills any human heart. Knowing that we can "change" another person through kind treatment, through taking an interest in what concerns them, is a precious good that motivates, satisfies and makes both sides happier.

But achieving that culture of service takes a whole process of education, motivation and civility. It takes clear sight. It takes being clear about the true purposes of the organization or the community. It takes knowing, or being convinced of, what gives sense and meaning to human work and human relationships. "The customer comes first" and "We are ladies and gentlemen serving ladies and gentlemen" are phrases built on a clear conviction of what it means to serve; they are premises that guide action. They are the "philosophy" behind a way of doing things. But they are not enough to create a culture of service.

A community or organizational culture centered on the real needs of the "customer-citizen" begins to exist when it is first embodied in a leader, or in a few leaders, who "preach" the need to "serve" with their words and their concrete actions. Their lives, at home and at work, rest on deep convictions and on a solid consistency between thought and action.

Leadership and service are synonyms. The authentic leader is "good" and "effective" when his purposes are humanizing and when he is also a living example of those purposes; he protects them and communicates them constantly, in simple language that everyone understands.

"Authentic leadership" and "service" are synonyms, and that inseparable pair is in turn a synonym of "humility", of small things done day by day, minute by minute. Being a leader does not only mean being the boss, the councilman or the manager. No. A leader is anyone who contributes creatively and responsibly from their own small post, perhaps without glamour or importance. In this way we can see that a culture of service is the fruit of a community of small leaders who are proactive, generous, cheerful and patriotic. They are the result of effective education, or of sound motivation carried out conscientiously by other leaders: a good boss, a mother, a friend, an authentic politician. An authentic culture of service is the result of a community that educates itself through exemplary actions, through effective but not manipulative strategies, with clarity of purpose, and with heart and mind set on what a human being, a good employee, a good politician and a good citizen really are.
