The family has always been recognized as a natural institution where people are worth more for what they are than for what they have. In the family each of us is always a person, a valuable subject, appreciated and treated as such. If one of us comes home tired from work or with a problem, it is usually in the family that we share it or let it show. And that happens because we are loved and valued beyond the roles we play in society. At home, as a rule, nobody calls us "stupid", "fools", "insecure" or "half-wits" (even though any of those things might be true). On the contrary, they look at us and accept us with our faults and our good qualities, and they put our small good qualities above our faults.

But someone will tell me that it is not like that in their family. True. But the exception is not a rule that allows us to spread the mistaken idea that this is how it is in "every" family in the world. Let us go on. Capitalist society, too, has long thought of the person more as a factor of production than as a human being with needs and potential that go beyond productive efficiency. But that same capitalist society has taken steps to try to change that view. Elton Mayo's psycho-sociological experiments early in this century began to show the existence of "informal or spontaneous relationships" among people working in a factory. In trying to measure which working conditions improve productivity, it was found that they did not depend on factors such as ventilation or lighting. Instead, "friendship", "informality" and "knowing they were doing something for the common good" produced results that amazed the whole scientific community of the time. It was these deeply human aspects that improved the productivity of the group being studied. Since then, industry and management have tried to "emulate" what often happens in families. Values such as "service", "trust", "unity" and "loyalty" are managed as goals that foster better relationships within the company. But one may ask: is life in a company always family-like and peaceful, touching and kind, however good its management of unifying values?

In my view, however hard this society tries, the "leadership of the family" still prevails, as the only human group united by strict bonds of love, and in which, therefore, a person is worth what he or she "is". In the family, being comes before having, and the person before things. Juan "is", María "is" and Antonio "is": in my family they are my son, my husband, my sister, my nephew. They "are", and they are worth more than any product with added value. Their personalities and traits are unique and original, and they stand above anything else that capitalist society measures as "useful". My children are not "useful" things or "efficient" beings. My children are beings to love, to understand, to encourage, to listen to and to care for, never to leave alone when they are sad. That, at least for me, is what makes the family a "leading" group: one that guides, encourages and "advises" with spontaneity and calm, or that sometimes demands firmly when necessary. And they do all this, moreover, without charging professional fees.

We must value and accept this obvious reality, and even if there are problems at times, there in my family there is almost always a refuge of peace, of light, of joy. My wife knows that little dish I love. My youngest son asks me to fix a broken toy and together we are happy. Playing the clown now and then makes my children, my wife and me far happier than going out for a few drinks with my buddies. Are there leading companies? Yes: my family, "the family".
