Going to pick up your father at his house because he asked you to print out a document for him (he doesn't have a printer, of course). Finding the house tidy, clean and even with new decorations because a visitor is expected. Offering a glass of fresh papaya juice. Hearing the neighbors ring the doorbell at 7:30 p.m. sharp, because we set the neighborhood association meeting for that time and committed to it. Greeting people kindly. Greeting them with a smile. Smiling. Asking for things with a "please" or a "could you?". Saying thank you for a favor, however small. Listening calmly to the other person. Going upstairs to say hello to grandmother, because she can't come down. Calling friends and relatives on their birthdays. Bringing flowers to your wife or your mother. Small touches.

Sometimes we lose our way, our calm, our peace and even our humanity, because we show none of these small touches. The city, the heat, the traffic and a thousand inconveniences make us sullen, unpleasant and even selfish. We care more about getting our goals done efficiently. We want to be more "effective" and less "human". We put above humanity and life the getting there first, the imposing ourselves on others because "I'm right", the eating and filling our stomachs on the dot, the carrying out of "our" plan exactly as foreseen, the not getting too tired because tomorrow I have to do this or that... and a long list of things that are urgent but not really important.

A *detalle*, in the second sense given by the dictionary of the Royal Spanish Academy, is a particular, a part or fragment of something. So a *detalle* is something small. And a *detalle*, in the third sense of the same dictionary, is a gesture of courtesy, kindness or affection. Something small and human that takes us out of ourselves and lets us give ourselves briefly to others. A momentary impulse that moves us to act for someone outside ourselves: an unknown passerby, a friend, a lady, an elderly person, a relative. We can say that such a gesture is a "transcendent" act that influences or changes other people in some way. And let us not forget that, as an inner and personal movement, it also changes and shapes us.

The small kindnesses I show others make me more human, because they help me grow and develop as a truly social, relational being, a "political animal". Small touches build immediate solidarity, the everyday kind, the real kind, not the kind that lies beyond our reach in faraway countries. Small touches make life more pleasant and psychologically healthier. They make us forget ourselves, cut off our dreams of vanity at the root, cut off the mental "reels" of our imagination and "unwind" them. Small touches help unfold imagination and creativity. Thinking of others, of what to give them, of how to help them, undoubtedly develops intelligence. It is normal and human to live by small touches, and it is something divine to come across people who are attentive to small touches in how they treat others and in what they do.
