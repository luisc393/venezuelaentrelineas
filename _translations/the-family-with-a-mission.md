Every time I open the fridge at home, I find a slightly yellowed piece of paper that says: "Whoever does not live to serve is not fit to live." My grandmother put it there, and since she was the one who reminded us of it all the time, it stayed engraved in my head and in my heart... It has often helped me to persevere in life and not to shrink in the face of obstacles. The anecdote shows how important a few values can be when they are written down, clear and simple: they can become the "true north" of each person's actions.

A family with a defined mission, with values that guide it, is not easy to find these days. More often we find cases like the one above: a relative somewhat more concerned with laying down rules of behavior. But in the world of family therapy today, it is being suggested that each family "build" its own mission according to its own needs and character (because every family is different), and because it has been found that a family mission has the power to give security and meaning. More than seven centuries ago, the theologian and philosopher Saint Thomas Aquinas wrote that parents are responsible for the promotion and guidance of their offspring. To speak of "guidance" is to speak of a process that is given a direction. And that "guidance" is reinforced by the idea of "promotion", which is deliberately oriented toward ends.

A family with a mission is a family with ends. And we can grasp those ends when, in a practical way, we ask ourselves a series of intelligent questions that help us think and reflect on them: What is the purpose of our family? What do we want from this family? Or, between husband and wife: What is the purpose of our marriage? What is its essential reason for being? What are our priorities? If we ask ourselves these questions tonight, in the silence before sleep, we may be surprised at how few clear answers we have. I believe we never reflect enough on these fundamental matters, perhaps because we think "there's no time", or it "isn't necessary", or it is "ridiculous", and so on. These excuses can only be overcome by an intense, serious and determined desire to truly improve.

But where to begin? First, following S. Covey (1997), by exploring what each member of the family feels and thinks, and giving each one a part to play, with questions such as these: What is the purpose of our family? What kind of family do we want to be? What kinds of things do we want to do? What feeling do we want to have at home? What kind of relationships do we each want to have at home? How do we want to treat one another and speak to one another? What things are truly important in our family? What are our family's priorities? What are the talents and abilities of each member of our family? What are their responsibilities? What principles and values should we follow? Who are our heroes? What can we imitate in them? Which family inspires us and serves as an example, and why do we admire it? How can we contribute to and serve society as a family? The point is to put our cards on the table, openly and frankly.

Second, we should begin writing many drafts of the family's mission, gathering the opinions and feelings from the first step. The final mission will be the one that everyone likes and everyone understands perfectly. It does not have to be long. It can even be a poem, a saying or a verse from a song that holds the values everyone shares.

Finally, the mission must be used to stay on the path through life and not stray from it. If everyone took part in writing it, everyone will be committed to it. So, as in the example at the beginning, it should be placed where everyone can see it. Displayed in this way, the mission becomes "the north" or "the lighthouse" that guides and spurs us on so that we do not stray, do not fail, and find a safe harbor. Its principles will give security and meaning, help us overcome problems large and small, and help us find answers that bring a certain degree of happiness and calm, which we badly need in these times of moral, social and economic turbulence.

I will end with an example of a family with a mission: "Our family mission is: To love one another, to help one another, to believe in one another. To use our time, talents and resources wisely to make others happy. To work together. Always..."
