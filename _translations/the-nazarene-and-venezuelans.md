This month of March, Lent begins. "Remember that you are dust, and to dust you shall return": that is the phrase that comes with the cross traced on our foreheads every Ash Wednesday. A hard, strong phrase that shakes us up, that strikes us in the soul and in the heart, because we know with absolute certainty that all of us (from the humblest to the most powerful) will end up as dust. Because that is the only sure thing we have in life: death.

For those of us who are Christians, thanks to God and to the Virgin, the subject of death is not so hard, because we have been taught since childhood that True Life begins after death, and that what matters is to strive hard, here on earth, to win Heaven and to make sure we never, not even as a joke, end up in Hell.

Lent is a wonderful time to reflect on this Number One subject of Life and Death, and I think it is fabulous that this year the Archdiocese of Caracas decided to take the venerated image of the Nazarene of San Pablo on a journey through different parts of Caracas. (To read what this Lenten Project 2011 is about, you can read the Lenten Proclamation on the website www.arquidiocesisdecaracas.com.)

Now you will ask me: and what does the title of this article have to do with what I have said so far?

Well, simply that this Lent is a wonderful opportunity for each of us Venezuelan Catholics to draw closer to the Nazarene; and that when we stand before that perfect wooden carving, we may look closely, with the eyes of the soul, at each of the marks of suffering of our Lord Jesus Christ, who tells us as he carries the cross: "To save you, I had to suffer this torment." Yes: "To save Me; not the world or the other sinners, but Me, who am His child, and who have also sinned."

May we, when we see the symbols of the passion and death of our Lord Jesus Christ that the Nazarene shows us, be able to say to Him: Lord, here I am to help you carry the Cross, carrying mine and helping others day by day, without complaining! Lord, I too want to drink the cup you drank and learn to live for others! Lord, may I know how to wear my daily crown of thorns, which is nothing compared to the one you wore, with joy and hope! Lord, may the whip with which you were so cruelly scourged teach me how to offer up my daily pains and setbacks for the forgiveness of sinners and for Peace in my beloved homeland! Lord, may I keep those three nails that were driven so savagely into you on the cross present in my mind and in my heart every time I am tempted to offend you! My God, may the spear that pierced your side teach me that the most horrible thing in the world is violence and hatred, and may its image help me become an unwavering defender of life, of peace and of harmony among all my fellow Venezuelans! And may the hyssop with which they gave you vinegar teach me that I came to this earth to serve and not to be served, just as You taught us!

These are just some of the questions we can ask ourselves before the Nazarene to examine ourselves better this Lent and then make a wonderful Confession.

That way we will be ready in case we are called early for the final journey.

But it doesn't end there. That is just where it begins, because, as the Lenten Proclamation of the Archdiocese of Caracas says:

"We are called to be missionary disciples of Jesus, to share his life with our people.

Being his disciples means following him, living in communion with him, imitating his example, bearing witness to a Christian life and boldly proclaiming his message.

Each person's response to this call of Jesus Christ and his Church is decisive for improving family life and for growing in social harmony.

To practicing Catholics we say: join the Continental Mission of Evangelization! God gives us all the chance to take part in proclaiming our faith, which is a treasure we must share with others. The risen Jesus sends us: 'Go and make disciples of all nations' (Mt 28:19-20)."

May God, through this image of the Nazarene, say many things to our hearts this Lent that stir us and move us quickly to follow the call of Our Lord Jesus Christ to be his missionary disciples. Because He needs each one of us to carry out his plan of salvation. Let us not let Him down. Let us behave as the exemplary children of God that we are: VENEZUELANS!
