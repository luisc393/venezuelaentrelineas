Behind the cameras and the big headlines of the national press, behind every artist and every politician of the moment turned celebrity; behind the big boss, the big manager, the minister, the governor, the mayor, the councilman; behind the loudest, the liar, the petty, the hypocrite, the one who knows everything and predicts everything; behind the rich man, opulent and distant, behind each of those famous people that today's society enthrones... there is that other group of anonymous heroes without whom no country can be built and sustained. These men and women are almost hidden by the false noise of those who think they are the makers of history. And it turns out that the true history of a nation is made day by day, minute by minute, in a fabric woven out of small things, small decisions, small customs and habits, reflections, heroic deeds, heroic silences, heroic listening...

Every day our beloved Venezuela wakes up to the love of a mother who looks after her family with sacrifice and without expecting anything in return; it wakes up to the willpower of a man who goes out to earn the money that pays for daily life and for his children's schooling and food. Every day a friend, or a close relative, tells us something affectionate and full of meaning that encourages us and stirs us inside. Every day a firefighter or a police officer, a shopkeeper or an athlete, a priest or a mailman helps paint the picture of a better country. Every day someone is the small protagonist without the show: the one who offers a service, the one who works with their hands to fix something broken, the one who puts their shoulder to the wheel to work as a team.

Every day each of them makes mistakes and tries to correct them, and keeps going in the hope that things can change. Every day a new Venezuelan is born, someone of whom we expect a great deal, and every day someone dies, leaving us a furrow of small good deeds done with the best possible effort...

This is the Venezuela unknown to and ignored by so many. The Venezuela of the genuine hero. The hero as Augusto Mijares defined him: the one who resists when others give in; who believes when others doubt; who rebels against routine and conformity. This is the "strong" Venezuela that we must rediscover, honor and encourage. In it lies our greatest hope. In it lies the inspiration that lights the way, guides, orients, shapes and pushes forward. And I firmly believe that each of us carries inside that essential heroism which makes it possible to build a better country.

Each of us is capable of resisting indifference and selfishness, empty noise and constant manipulation. Each of us is capable of believing that one day we will have a better country, because we try to put enthusiasm into what we do and pass that same enthusiasm on to others, teaching first by our own conduct.

Each of us, moreover, is capable of being a great rebel, because we do not stop, do not grow sad, do not freeze; we trust fully, use our imagination, observe with the heart, try to break the mold, search, inquire, investigate, and want, with the power of our will and the light of our intelligence, to change things. In each of us, then, is that "other" Venezuela.
