Some people find it a little hard to understand why Venezuelans are so joyful.

Several months ago an Italian who had come to our country for work told us that he had been terrified of moving to Venezuela. His family and friends told him he was "crazy" to accept his company's transfer to such a dangerous Third World country.

He says that what struck him most when he arrived at the airport was seeing so many smiling, helpful people. And he kept seeing the same thing in the streets, in the people who welcomed him, in his coworkers (who always had a little joke ready and loved to tease him), and so on. He couldn't understand how it was possible that Venezuelans, with so many problems (which of course he heard about all day long... and they told him about them all day long...), could be so joyful.

And it is true that some things are hard to understand. For example, when you look at the world's suicide statistics, you wonder: why does Sweden, the "most developed" country in the world (they even heat the streets so that Swedes don't know what cold is), have one of the highest suicide rates in the world: 18.1 men and 8.3 women per 100,000 inhabitants?

But notice that there is indeed a link between what one can observe and the numbers in the statistics. For example, someone who was in Austria recently told me about the sadness he saw in Austrians' faces. And looking at the statistics, we can see why: the suicide rate in Austria is 13.8 men and 7.4 women per 100,000 inhabitants.

In Switzerland (another "super developed" country, where everything is settled and planned before you are born, they even insure your reading glasses), the suicide rate is 13.5 men and 11.7 women.

Our beloved Venezuela, on the other hand, has one of the lowest suicide rates in the world, with 6.1 men and 1.4 women per 100,000 inhabitants. As always, Venezuelan women set the example of strength and of being true "all-terrain" women, who despite the endless problems of daily life are happy, not to say "super happy".

I won't have room today to tell you all the reasons for this joy, but I promise I will, because there are quite a few, and I know you will like them very much.

Today I will speak of just one, which I think is the main one: our Faith. We Venezuelans are people of Faith, and we live with our Faith close at hand, day to day.

That same Italian I mentioned at the start of the article went last year to the procession of the Divina Pastora, and he told me: "I just can't believe it. It's one thing what people tell you and another what you see there, in person. I had never seen so many people with so much faith, and they were all happy, in unbearable heat."

Well, my dear friend, I answered, Venezuela has been blessed by God and has an impressive religious tradition. It is that tradition which, generation after generation, has helped us live as God's beloved children in this wonderful land (full of problems, it is true, but also full of good things), and which at this moment is asking us to live as true brothers and sisters and to keep praying and striving, harder than ever, to sow the Love, Peace and Justice that only God can give us.

And truly, the joy of Venezuelans is something that can only be understood in the presence of God.

How beautiful it was to see those millions of people (a very high percentage of them young) walking through streets that were burning with heat! Many of them didn't mind and walked barefoot. What would the Swedes say!

But the greatest and most beautiful thing was to see the joy overflowing from each of their hearts, because they were accompanying our Most Holy Virgin, under the title of the Divina Pastora, in the second-largest religious gathering of its kind in the world.

How great is our Faith! How great is our joy! How great it is that we Venezuelans know we are children of God! How great it is to love all our fellow Venezuelans! How great it is to pray and strive in our homeland for a better Venezuela for everyone!

*Published in El Universal (January 19, 2011).*
