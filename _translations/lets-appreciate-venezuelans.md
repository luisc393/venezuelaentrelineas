*Has anyone told today's Venezuelans what their qualities are, their good traits and their potential?*

*Does anyone, by any chance, praise their efforts, their original ways of doing things, their creativity, their warmth, their ease in communicating and in standing by others?*

**Every** Venezuelan has some positive ability that deserves applause, praise and recognition. Praising and appreciating others is necessary. When we are not busy thinking about some specific problem, we tend to spend 95 percent of our time thinking about ourselves. But if we stop thinking about ourselves for a while and begin to think about other people's good qualities, we will not need to resort to flattery, which is so cheap and so false that it is recognized the moment it leaves our lips. We feed our children and friends, our relatives, our coworkers; but we very rarely feed their self-esteem. We give them meat and potatoes so they have energy, but we neglect to give them kind words of appreciation that would sing in their memory for years.

Today's Venezuelans need their mistakes to be pointed out, they need to be challenged, they need to be told their weaknesses, but... has anyone told them what their qualities are, their good traits and their potential?

At work, do bosses and managers praise the person who does their job well, who is steady, who contributes something on their own initiative, who sets an example, who doesn't gossip? At school and at university, do teachers and professors praise students for their constructive and original participation, for their free and respectful opinions, for their effort to do better work or earn a good grade, for listening to their classmates? In politics and in public life generally, does any official praise what is positive and constructive in an opposing idea or opinion, commend and consider proposals from another party or from civil society, or thank organized groups and plain, ordinary people for the solutions and suggestions they offer?

We live in a young and democratic country, a country that must be built and developed by everyone, not by one man or by a group of men. We must leave envy behind and get rid of the personal pride of believing ourselves better than others, of thinking that someone wants to take my job or bring me down, of believing there is always an enemy at my side. Ralph Waldo Emerson said: "Every man I meet is in some way my superior; and in that I can learn of him." Venezuela needs us to stop thinking about our own achievements and needs. Rather, it needs us to try to think about the good qualities of others. Let us forget flattery, which is selfish, and instead give proof of honest and sincere appreciation of those qualities. Let us be hearty in our approval and lavish in our praise, and the Venezuelans around us will warmly welcome our words, which will surely encourage them to be better. Each and every one of us needs a little approval and self-esteem to come through the present crisis successfully.
