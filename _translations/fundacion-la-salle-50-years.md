*Fundación La Salle de Ciencias Naturales has spent more than fifty years serving Venezuela, its people and its environment. Going back a little into its history, we find how the assertive leadership of Brother Ginés led a group of enthusiastic young nature lovers to travel the length and breadth of the country, on foot, by jeep, by dugout canoe and by plane, collecting plants and animals and recording Venezuela's indigenous cultures for history. But since a scientific vocation cannot be separated from social commitment, in 1952 the foundations were laid for what is today the Fundación La Salle de Ciencias Naturales. Its three lines of action (Research, Education and Outreach-Production), based on its mission of reaching the most neglected communities, are a challenge for a team of 1,325 committed men and women who every day work hard to check that the Mission and social reality match.*

*Brother Ginés, founder of the Fundación La Salle de Ciencias Naturales (FLASA). It is thanks to his enterprising spirit that Venezuela today has a great institution devoted to studying the country's renewable natural resources and to human development through educational institutions.*

**Fundación** La Salle is present in Punta de Piedras (Nueva Esparta State), San Carlos and El Baúl (Cojedes State), Boconó (Trujillo State), Timotes (Mérida State), San Félix and Tumeremo (Bolívar State), and Punta Pescador and Muaina (Delta Amacuro State). Inspired by the charism of John Baptist de La Salle and with a very particular institutional philosophy, it has worked for more than 50 years for the regions where it is based: researching their natural resources to put them at the service of the people who live there, rooting them in their culture and so helping to reduce the exodus to the capital.

### Applied, regional research

Research is one of the most important pillars of the Fundación La Salle. It regards science, in general terms, as humanity's most important intellectual creation for interpreting its object, nature, and using it rationally and responsibly. At the same time, research at the Fundación La Salle sees scientific activity as a key element of the country's technological independence and development, and, by involving the people who benefit, it encourages the transfer of research results for direct application in production.

Research at the Fundación La Salle recognizes the scientific method as the great instrument that guarantees the reliability of all science; yet it also values empirical knowledge as a contribution to meeting people's needs locally.

In the social sciences, research at the Fundación La Salle values cultures of oral tradition and works to strengthen and recover their mechanisms and everything that is handed down through them, thereby helping to strengthen national identity.

To carry out all these aims, the institution has five scientific research stations, the oceanographic ship *Hno. Ginés*, two natural science museums, a planetarium, a Caribbean institute of anthropology and sociology, and a floating fish farm, all coordinated by trained scientific and technical staff.

### Education for life: applying knowledge and learning by doing

Education is another great pillar of the Fundación La Salle. It runs technical high schools and technical university institutes that train young people according to the needs of their region. Aquaculture, oceanography, navigation and fishing, and food technology are some of the programs taught on Margarita Island. In the plains, the Fundación La Salle trains young people in agriculture and livestock programs, as it also does in the Andes; in Guayana, the programs focus on industry, mining and forestry. It currently has about 12,000 students and more than 13,000 graduates. It also serves young people who have dropped out of formal education, training them in short technical programs that help them enter the job market.

### Outreach and production: reaching out to the community

The Fundación La Salle has a third line of action, outreach and production, which it carries out through:

- 11 documentation centers and archives located on all its campuses.
- Various small businesses that meet the needs of each region.
- The Comprehensive Guidance Service (SOI), in charge of the institutional philosophy, and
- FLASA Producciones, an audiovisual center that spreads environmental messages through television.

Among its future projects, the Fundación La Salle has the Museum of Venezuelan Man and His Environment and the Universidad La Salle del Caribe, both already more than 50% advanced.

And with the same mottos that inspired Brother Ginés and his collaborators back in the 1940s, **"To God through Nature"** and **"With work, everything can be achieved"**, the Fundación La Salle keeps doing its part for the full development of Venezuela.
