*Fundación Bigott celebrates more than two decades of its Popular Culture Workshops.*

*In the historic center of Petare, right across from Plaza Sucre, stands the house that is home to the Fundación Bigott. Since 1982 the foundation has been researching, promoting and spreading Venezuela's cultural traditions through its Popular Culture Workshops program, which is now 21 years old and serves the community by supporting hundreds of students who want to learn and practice Venezuela's artistic traditions.*

*The Workshops aim to foster knowledge and recognition of the cultural expressions that make up our country's living heritage. To that end, they work every day to cultivate and promote popular traditions through a range of subjects such as singing, dance, the making of musical instruments (lutherie) and traditional costume.*

### Twenty years of growth

**In** 1982 "Voz y Visión", an advertising firm specializing in corporate communication and headed by Teresa Zapata, took on the general coordination of the Popular Culture Workshops, leaving the teaching coordination to Rafael Salazar, who at the time was Secretary General of the National Federation of Popular Culture (FENACUP). Salazar designed the initial work plan and brought together the first group of teachers, made up of specialist instructors and traditional artists.

The first workshops taught mask making, the dances of Corpus Christi, the *cuatro* and creativity for children. Soon afterward a six-month course in the *bandola* was opened, and in September of that same year a concert was given by the students who had completed it.

By 1984 the number of workshops had grown, and eight subjects were offered: dance accessories, harp, *bandola*, *cuatro*, dance, guitar, mandolin and percussion. That same year the dance group "Vivencias Populares" was created, and the first parade of Calypso troupes was held, made up of students and musical groups from the Workshops.

In 1989 the Fundación Bigott hired the sociologist Tulio Hernández to evaluate the educational work of the Workshops; his assessment found that "the most decisive and widely shared result was the high value, esteem and recognition that the Fundación Bigott enjoys among its users".

In 1990, under the direction of the sociologist María Teresa López, the Popular Culture Workshops began a stage of building their theoretical foundations, continuing and reinforcing the teaching, structuring the design of the instruction programs and drawing up the project for a Documentation Center, among other activities that consolidated what we know today as the Popular Culture Workshops. María Teresa is currently in charge of showcasing the work done in the workshops and, above all, coordinates the efforts of the Foundation's musical groups.

### A school for many arts and crafts: teaching

Also at the start of the nineties, the educator José Esteban Pérez, from Lara State, joined the team of the Popular Culture Workshops. From the beginning he grasped the essence of the project: "we try to pass on, as well as we possibly can, the rich experience that comes from Venezuela's traditional genres, through programs designed with educational criteria, across three levels: children, young people and adults."

For José, now the Fundación Bigott's teaching coordinator, the work done through the Popular Culture Workshops goes beyond researching and spreading Venezuelan traditions. "Its importance lies in how current and adaptable the programs are for today's times, which has led us to organize formats that respond to contemporary educational needs and interests." Some of these services are:

- Workshops by modules
- Short workshops
- Open workshops

In addition, a series of other activities is offered:

- Vacation program
- Recreational visits
- Workshops for teachers, recreation leaders and communicators
- Student advising
- Wednesday coffee gatherings and full-moon evenings

### Teaching that reaches out

The Popular Culture Workshops are the seedbed of several musical groups that bring Venezuelan folklore to the public while also showcasing the teaching done within the institution.

"Our performance groups help present our many cultural and popular traditions to Venezuelans and foreigners in contemporary formats." "With our groups we have had the chance to perform in different regions of the country, before very diverse audiences, and also to represent Venezuela at important international gatherings," said María Teresa López. Three groups currently belong to the Fundación Bigott: Vasallos del Sol, Pasacalle and Pomarrosa.
