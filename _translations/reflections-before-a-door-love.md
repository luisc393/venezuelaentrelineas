*[Drawing: an open door with a flowerpot on the threshold.]*

It may seem strange that I, who am always stopping humorously before doors, should choose today the door of love, a door that does not lend itself to irony, because the highest of human feelings must be treated with respect. So here I am, before this door of love, and without humor. At least I hope so, since in today's world it is very easy to catch that sly lightness with which every subject is treated. Let us trust that I will stay serious.

Love is a constructive force; yes, it is creative. Even in its material stage, when it elevates the biological function of continuing the species through the union of the sexes. It creates flesh and soul in the human species, unless some sad little pill has come to interrupt its noble cycle. Nothing is more opposed to love than these pills or other methods for the same end, because love must always be a whole, not a part, as so many believe today. The world is mistaken when it thinks it is favoring love by avoiding procreation. It wants to give the name of love only to the momentary pleasure of bodies in an unfinished instant of the process of love. Where there is no creation there is no love, just as we could not say there is agriculture if we watered the fields only to pull up the plants afterward. Love without children is incomplete; so much so that a human couple to whom God does not give fruit of flesh and soul should direct this creative force into work that gives them children of the spirit. Love has a function of perpetuity that can only be ignored through monstrous selfishness. What is more, selfishness itself is a monster born of a deviation of this force: love of oneself. Love always creates, and if it is diverted, it will create monsters.

Nowadays people make fun of love. Romantics hide the fact because they are considered corny. People say with a smug little smile: "I don't believe in love." The heroines of the last century who died of tuberculosis and in love are out of fashion. And yet we have a great need for love. Psychologists and psychiatrists never tire of repeating the behavioral disorders that arise in adults as a result of a lack of love in childhood. The literature and films of our time insist on glorifying, inadequately, it is true, but they do it, something they call love: sexual relations. They speak of free love. As we said, it is an incomplete aspect of love that seems to worry people, interest them and even destroy them. Deep down it is because there is such a great need for love in the soul of today's man, devoured by his selfishness, by this negative love of himself, that he wants to find the eternity of the feeling through its poorest but most comfortable form: the pleasure of the flesh. And of course he finds no peace, because he has missed his destiny. His immortal soul cannot be satisfied with passing pleasures. The love of the flesh is as fleeting as the love of the spirit is eternal.

What happens to today's man is that he takes a shot at love with the wrong aim. As if, at the rocket bases aiming for the moon, they tried to reach it by pointing at the earth. It is not a matter of a bank shot, as in billiards. To go to the moon you have to fire "toward the moon". And it is the same with love. You have to aim at Love with a capital L, which is God. No stage of love is fulfilled on earth if its final direction does not point to its center. But we do not want to understand this, and we plow the sea. We fire at the bed, and the shot falls so short that it rebounds and destroys our own heart. That is why so many men walk the streets, hold posts, public offices, teaching jobs and positions of every kind, without creating anything. They go about with an empty chest.

*Alicia Álamo Bartolomé*
