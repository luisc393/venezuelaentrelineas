One of the legacies left by the generation of fervent feminists of the sixties to the eighties was the idea of a woman's "fulfillment". They held, with a sweeping air that so often covers up a fallacy, that in order to "fulfill herself" a woman had to "free herself from the home". They conjured up the figure of a sad, frustrated housewife among dirty diapers and piles of dishes to wash, brooding over some dream of triumph in business or public life.

That generalization has spread extraordinarily. It led, almost like an epidemic, many women to question their own role in the home. And that seed, watered by a lack of reflection and the tendency to do "what everybody does", has grown and borne bitter fruit: the loss of one's own identity, rootlessness.

For men as for women, the home is their own sphere, not something foreign to our nature. The social dimension of a man, or a woman, begins with making a home: the house is four walls; the home that the two of them manage to make is the human, intangible dimension, because it goes beyond what is material. The home is not incompatible with other social spheres; it comes before them and goes along with them. The school, the company, the factory, the workshop, the farm... How much they owe to the homes of the men and women who work there!

How much one devotes to the home and to those other possible activities is always a personal decision. In a woman's case, to her honor and glory, choosing the home, that is, placing it at the top of the ranking of her time and effort, is enormously important. We have just said that the home is made by both the man and the woman. But not in the same way. Here, too, the difference between the sexes is clearly seen. A woman is psychologically better prepared to give the home its cohesion: her flexibility and ability to adapt, her common and practical sense, her love of detail, her tenderness and capacity for self-sacrifice are irreplaceable, and what is not given in quantity is given in quality.

This dedication of a woman to the home is not simply a question of roles: it is not a division of labor in which, the fervent feminists would say, women got the worst part. Indeed, changing diapers, taking out the trash or going to the supermarket is not "feminine", just as paying the bills, taking the children to school or tending the garden is not "masculine". A family, a home, does not run on stereotypes; life is very rich, and many tasks call for whoever is at hand, whether man or woman. But this much is true: each carries them out as what he or she is, with a man's hand or with feminine genius. There is no room here for an exchange of roles: we are dealing with the very reality of one's being. That is what is meant by the qualitative dedication of the wife and mother, of the daughter in her parents' home, of the feminine presence (the grandmother, the aunt...) in another home that she will always make her own.

Is staying at home "frustration" and working outside it "fulfillment"? We said that the generalization is irritating. Every person should have a plan for life, and fulfillment is the unfolding of that plan. Often the plan matures and even changes radically: an illness, the death of loved ones or financial setbacks can alter its course. Accepting with maturity the changes, perhaps irreversible, that such circumstances bring, and adjusting our plan as we go, is not frustration if in each case we keep hold of the reins of our life by our own free choice. Staying at home is neither better nor worse than going out to work. Inside and outside I will have challenges, happy moments and days full of difficulties; not everything will be rosy, either at home or at the office; unpleasant or less agreeable tasks await me in both places; there will also be long faces, scoldings and impatience in the law office or the operating room, just as there are in my own home. We do not live in Master More's Utopia: what is real is that I am always a woman, at every moment, and there are always people who expect the best of me. Will we come to realize this more and more, seeking a calm and joyful acceptance of our own identity, so that we can give the other half of the world the gift of the "feminine genius" that has earned so much praise?
