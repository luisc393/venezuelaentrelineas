When we speak of the will to achieve things, to study, to work, to face challenges, to change situations, we mean that kind of resolve, drive and determination that moves us to carry out actions we have planned and decided on. We forget, however, that such resolve goes through an earlier process of preparation that helps define its direction and its final achievement. Without that earlier space, no decision is possible. Why? Because the human will needs inner strength, and that strength can only be built by clearing away a series of obstacles that we create for ourselves out of a false idea of what we are capable of.

This inner strength, which makes it possible to take decisions and to build an iron will to change, is the result of education. Educating what is inside us, what moves us, our will, first requires getting rid of an attitude that is very fashionable these days: "thoughtlessness". With this attitude we don't think about what we are going to do, and we let ourselves be carried along by the impulse of the moment, by passion, by routine, by whim. We must therefore stop to consider before acting, and see whether this is what we ought to do. Having some time to think is an indispensable human need for making good personal decisions.

Second, there is another illness of our time, "feverish haste", which, by creating tension that is too strong and badly governed, uselessly wears out body and soul and leads us astray toward the wrong things. Calm and moderation are therefore needed so that our work does not go up in smoke. One way to overcome this crisis lies in putting our activities in order and actually getting done, without delay, everything we have to do. That is why punctuality in our activities, from the moment we get up until the end of the day, is vital to getting rid of any overwhelming haste.

Third, there is "hesitation" or "indecision", also called laziness or lack of moral energy, which paralyzes or withers the strength of the will. We are indecisive, we are afraid, and deep down we don't want to commit to change, to resolve, to determination. "What will people say" holds us back and paralyzes us. Perhaps the obstacles mentioned above have not been truly removed. There has been no real inner commitment, of mind and heart, to the decision taken. We have lacked courage.

Finally, there is the "fear of failure", or lack of confidence, which drains our strength. We must keep in mind that, with God's help, we can be very successful. And if we have thought the matter through, and even talked it over with someone more experienced, we will generally succeed. Yet nothing is certain. We live without knowing what tomorrow will bring. We cannot know the unexpected turns of every situation or the inner intentions of every person. We must prepare ourselves for failure. What is more, failure becomes a gain, because we can learn a great deal, gain experience and reshape our future decisions. Let us not forget that one can gain, and gain a lot, by losing...

Let us take heart and educate our will, trying to remove these obstacles day by day, moment by moment. A will formed to move forward is the result of a long struggle. But it is a struggle that begins at some concrete moment and changes the course of our life story. As Gandhi once said: "If you want to progress, you must not repeat history, but make a new history."
