### The University a few years ago and now

When I went back to the University, eight years after leaving it, I found a very different picture. I had left a University City under construction. I had taken my courses in the makeshift classrooms of what would later become a student residence. Some semesters we even had to make do under a ramshackle shed that later disappeared. There were none of today's paved paths. Many of us students reached the old Hacienda Ibarra by crossing scrubland and a little wooden bridge over the Guaire River, after the bus dropped us off at Plaza Venezuela. Getting to class, then, was something of a country adventure. There were few of us girls, at least in the Faculty of Engineering, to which the School of Architecture then belonged. The fashion we wore suited the surroundings we moved in. Never high heels, and very rarely stockings. Morning clothes washed over and over and ironed. Makeup and hairdresser: zero. Nobody wore the famous "teased" hair that today adds height to many a girl around here, and so the beauty salons got along perfectly well without our weekly visit. The boys themselves dressed quite simply, in khaki and without ties; if you saw one, it was around the neck of some professor, since even among them the sporty, hiking style prevailed.

Some years later, things have changed completely. On our old grasslands, where we did our surveying practice, now rise the slender buildings born of the mind and hand of Carlos Raúl Villanueva, one of my professors. I very much enjoyed being a student at the University City again for four years (which have also gone by now) and walking along the boldly shaped concrete corridors, in the shade that we students of yesteryear did not have. There I found not only that my fellow women students had multiplied many times over, but a new generation of students with a completely different outlook. We were simple pioneers of a university campus that was just being born. Now this new generation of women students, who have taken possession of the proud modern architecture, carries itself as if at a fashion show. I was surprised to find, early in the morning, more than one student looking like a fashion plate. Not only the exquisite dress, in fabric, cut and style, but careful makeup and hair arranged in complicated shapes that told me of getting up very early to look the part. I myself began to worry about how I looked in the morning. Had I gone to class as in my good old days, I would have looked like a country girl just out of her corn husk. I looked at my new classmates' feet and remembered our dusty moccasins with nostalgic humor. Now the granite rings like castanets under the heels of a girl walking briskly by.

### Let us be a little critical

I admit that at first I felt something like an instinctive rejection of the new students' exaggerated elegance. Worrying about fashion did not seem to suit the business of studying. Especially at such an early hour. Later I understood that the civilized setting, which we did not have, set the tone for how people dressed. Late arrivals have increased at the new University; getting ready takes time. We often arrived late too; we spent less time getting ready, but we slept more, and our journey was not usually shortened by a private car.

Someone asked me to write this article about fashion at the University. I don't know whether I started well with this comparison between yesterday and today. Nor do I know how I am going to continue it, or end it.

I am no fashion expert. But I am a woman, and I think that goes a long way.

Have I got to the point? Let the reader judge. Have I said anything interesting? Another judgment that I partly leave to the reader, since I will get ahead and criticize myself. No, I have not said anything worthwhile. This article lacks criticism. Well-meant criticism, of course; no acid little remarks about the swaying feminine figures we bump into in the corridors and classrooms of the University City.

### Is there a "university fashion"?

All right, then, let us criticize. Let us begin with the positive side that all criticism should have, by answering this question: should a university student be fashionably dressed? Why, of course; a woman should always take care to look good wherever she is. Our student of today gets full marks on this point. Another question: is there such a thing as university fashion? Hmm...! One look around and we see such a variety of outfits that we cannot classify this fashion. We cannot even be sure of the day's weather when we start looking at the girls' clothes. I would say it is cold, because here comes one in a thick sweater with a loose, existentialist cut; but right behind her, with the short tapping of heels due to how little fabric there is in the skirt (both vertically and horizontally), comes another girl with a low neckline and no sleeves at all, who reminds me of beachwear. This confuses me. Along come others, cold enough for light jackets or warm enough for a sporty blouse or the so-called shirtwaist dress. They look good; I like their look better than that of the chilly one in the ski sweater or the hot one in the scanty fabric. But in the end, as regards personal temperatures, they seem to have nothing to do with the temperature of the surroundings.

### The troubles of the short skirt

I look into a class and see some girls trying hard, without success, to show their knees as little as possible to the professor. No, those skirts do not come down any further when one sits; for if, besides being narrow (so narrow!), even standing up they show more of what is beyond than of what is below (below being the floor), when the girl sits down her forlorn knees end up far from the hem and mistresses of the air. That other, pleated skirt is more comfortable, since it lets one attend more to one's notes than to the exact position of one's lower limbs. Well, let us note one point in our study of what university fashion should be: comfort first, young ladies, together with peace of mind. Peace of mind for everyone, including the professor.

That other "model" in a cocktail dress looks very pretty, especially if she were at a cocktail party; but among the sober complicity of the classroom walls she shines like a star lost in the daytime. Even if it is already night at the University City. Because the growth of afternoon and evening courses has led many students to show up dressed as if for an afternoon reception. But there is a difference: it is one thing to be dressed for a reception and another to be dressed for the afternoon. At the University, as a rule, apart from graduations or some other celebration, there are only classes in the afternoon, not receptions. You go to receptions to socialize and to show off; you go to class to study and to socialize. To show off too, perhaps, but to look good in keeping with the place. There are students who, when you look at them, you expect to find holding the glass stem of a cocktail rather than the plastic of a ballpoint pen.

### And the hairstyles?

And the hairstyles? Ah, better not to talk about that; everyone can do whatever they please with their hair. The only thing is that now, when students take their seats in class, it would be best to switch roles and make it "gentlemen first", because the poor uglier sex will not be able to see the blackboard behind the towers of teased hair.

People often say that girl students go to class so elegantly dressed because their purpose in enrolling at the University is not exactly to finish a degree but to start a career in marriage. I don't believe this statement can be absolute (I am on my second degree and...), but if there is some truth in it, I don't think that intention deserves reproach. Everyone defends themselves as best they can.

But let us say something in fairness: these elegant, fashionable girls who now fill the classrooms of the University City, whatever their purpose, and let us respect it, mostly do not do badly in the course of their studies. The female students stand out for their good grades.

### Elegance and idleness are not synonyms

So elegance and idleness are not synonyms. We are glad to confirm it. It means that a woman's elegance in the classrooms where science, technology and the humanities are taught is a triumph of femininity. Gone is the image of slovenliness that men used to have of the woman scientist and of the university woman in general. This fashion, which has aroused the journalist's curiosity, comes today to prove it.

*Alicia Álamo Bartolomé*
